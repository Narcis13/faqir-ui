New in 1.3

One attribute gives any subtree a brand hue, in any theme.

data-accent re-hues the theme's accent for an element and everything inside it. The theme keeps its own lightness and chroma; only the hue moves. No theme is generated, nothing is recompiled, and a hue only joins the vocabulary once it has cleared every contrast pair the auditor checks, in every shipped theme, light and dark. 

<html data-accent="violet">                       … the whole page
<section data-accent="orange">                     … one region
<div data-accent="custom" style="--accent-hue: 200"> … any other hue

Any other hue is custom, and its contrast is yours.

data-accent="custom" takes its hue from --accent-hue, in degrees or as an angle. The theme cannot know that hue in advance, so nothing measures it. The presets below include the greens and blues the gate left out of the named list; read the primary button's label on each before you ship one.

Incident

Five tokens move. Everything else stays the theme's.

The five tokens the theme studio's accent dial writes are re-pointed with relative colour syntax over the theme's own value of each, so their lightness and chroma are exactly what the theme declared.

TokenBecomes
--color-primaryoklch(from <theme value> l c <hue>)
--color-primary-hoverthe same, over the theme's hover
--color-primary-activethe same, over the theme's active
--color-primary-subtlethe same, over the theme's subtle fill
--color-ringthe same, over the theme's focus ring
  • --color-primary-fg, the label on a primary button, keeps the theme's ink. That is why the named hues were measured rather than picked.
  • --focus-ring-color and --doc-accent, the one ink a print theme draws in, are restated inside the accent so they follow it. A print theme's darker @media print ink keeps its lightness and takes the hue.
  • Everything sits inside @supports (color: oklch(from light-dark(red, red) l c h)). A browser without relative colour syntax renders the theme's own accent: the attribute degrades to a no-op, never to a page without a primary colour.
  • On an element that also carries data-theme or data-skin, the accent starts at that element's children; the element's own border or fill keeps the theme's accent.
Measured

A closed vocabulary, chosen by measurement.

Seventeen candidates, the conventional hue names, were re-hued into every shipped theme in both schemes and run through every pair contrast-tokens checks: the accent as text on the muted and subtle plates, the primary label on the primary fill, and the focus ring on every surface. Out-of-gamut results were measured twice, clipped and chroma-reduced. Nine hues pass in every case; four themes' light primary was darkened by the smallest step that let rose, red, orange, blue and indigo in.

Amber clears the gate by a hair and is left out on purpose; yellow and the green-to-sky arc fail in nine to twelve themes each. Adding a hue is additive under the protocol's amendment rules and has to pass the same test. The attribute itself is defined in Protocol 1.0, §4.

In your own page

Already in every bundle.

The implementation is one stylesheet, tokens/accent.css, which every token bundle and every faqir init project includes. A project that copies token files one by one imports it before density.css. 

faqir init --theme aurora
# then, anywhere in the page:
<body data-accent="indigo">
